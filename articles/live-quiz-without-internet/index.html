How to run a live class quiz with no internet (a free Kahoot alternative)

By Rabii Hajoui, English teacher in Morocco · 7 November 2026

In my school in Morocco, the Wi-Fi is often slow or missing, and most students have no mobile data. Apps like Kahoot and Quizizz are great, but every phone needs the internet. So the quiz stops before it starts.

The good news: a live quiz does not need the internet. It only needs the phones and the teacher's device to be on the same small network inside the classroom. Here is how I do it every week.

The idea: a small classroom network

Your phone's hotspot makes a Wi-Fi network. You can turn mobile data off and the network still works. Your laptop and your students' phones join it, and they talk to each other directly, inside the room. Nothing goes to the internet, so it costs nothing and it is fast.

What you need

  • A Windows laptop or an Android phone with MI WAY Classroom (free).
  • Students' phones: Android or iPhone, with any browser. No app, no account.
  • A hotspot (your phone), the school Wi-Fi, or a small Wi-Fi router.
  • A projector is nice, but not needed.

Step by step

  1. Turn on your hotspot. Mobile data can stay off.
  2. Connect your laptop to the hotspot and open MI WAY Classroom.
  3. Open Connect phones. A big QR code appears.
  4. Students join the hotspot and scan the QR code. They tap their name.
  5. Start the quiz. Answers arrive live on your screen. At the end, a podium shows the winners.

How many students can join?

Be careful: most phone hotspots allow only about 10 devices. Here is what works:

  • In your hotspot settings, look for "Maximum connections" and raise it, if your phone has it.
  • Put students in pairs or small teams, one phone per team. It is also good for speaking.
  • For 30–40 phones, use a cheap pocket Wi-Fi router. It does not need a SIM card or internet.
  • The Windows laptop hotspot allows 8 phones by default.

Tips from my class

  • No copying: every question has 4 versions, so two neighbours do not get the same sentence.
  • Test, teach, test again: start with a short quiz, teach what most students got wrong, then play again. The progress is clear for everyone.
  • Keep it short: 8–10 questions is enough. My students ask every day: "Sir, are we playing today?"
