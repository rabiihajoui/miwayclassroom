Test-Teach-Test: a ready English lesson plan (with a free quiz to start)

By Rabii Hajoui, English teacher in Morocco · 8 November 2026

For years I taught the same way: I explained the grammar, then students practised. But many students already knew half of it, and others were lost from the start. Test-Teach-Test changed my lessons. I start with a short test, I teach only what the class got wrong, and I finish with a second test so everyone sees their progress.

What is Test-Teach-Test?

It has three steps:

  1. Test 1: a short quiz before you explain anything. It shows what students already know.
  2. Teach: you explain only the points that most students missed.
  3. Test 2: a new quiz on the same point. Students see their progress, and so do you.

Compared with "present, practise, produce", you lose less time on what students already know, and the lesson follows your class, not the book.

A 50-minute lesson plan

Example: Present simple (Outlook, Unit 2 — Common Core). It works the same for any lesson.

TimeStepWhat you do
5 minWarm-upTwo questions about the students' day: "What time do you wake up?"
10 min① Test 110 questions on students' phones. Don't explain anything yet.
5 minLook at resultsLook at the questions most students missed, for example the -s with he/she/it.
15 min② TeachExplain only those points. Use 3–4 examples from the students' answers.
10 min③ Test 210 new questions on the same point.
5 min📊 CompareShow Test 1 and Test 2 side by side. Celebrate the progress!

Tips

  • Tell students that Test 1 is not for marks. It's a "let's see what we know" game. They relax and answer honestly.
  • Keep the teaching short. If Test 1 was very good, go straight to Test 2 or to speaking.
  • Every question has 4 versions, so Test 2 doesn't repeat Test 1 word for word, and neighbours can't copy.
